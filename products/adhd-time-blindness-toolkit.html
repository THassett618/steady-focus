The problem this is for

Many people with ADHD describe losing track of how much time has passed, often called "time blindness". One clinic's write-up describes it like this:

"You sit down to answer one email, and an hour disappears. You meant to leave the house at 8:00, and somehow it is 8:40."Talkiatry, ADHD and time blindness

The same page lists two practical tips that this toolkit puts on paper: "Add 50% to every time estimate" and set alarms "for when to stop, not just when to start".

What is inside

  1. How to use (a quick start)
  2. Time Block Card: visible time anchors instead of a blank grid
  3. Stop-Alarm Checklist: what to do the moment time got away from you
  4. Time-Cost Estimator: pads every estimate by 50% before you commit

Delivered as one combined PDF plus separate files.

How it is meant to be used

  • Guess how long a job will take. Write it in the Estimator and see the padded number.
  • Put the padded time on the Time Block Card so you can see it.
  • Use the Stop-Alarm Checklist for the moment you realise you have lost track.

Who it is not for

  • It does not make the clock easier to feel. It plans around the fact that it can be hard to feel. If that does not describe you, skip it.
  • It is paper and has no alarms of its own. You still need a phone or timer for the stop alarm. If you want a visual-timer app, our comparison guide explains when that is the better choice.
