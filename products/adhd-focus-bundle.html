The problem this is for

One writer who tried several ADHD planners described a $35 one as having "pages and pages of work ahead" and found it too bulky to want to carry. Another writer on planning put the general point plainly:

"If your planner is a lot of work to keep up with, you're probably not going to stick with it."Neurodivergent Notes, "Why ADHDers fail at planning"

The same piece names fragmented systems as a problem too: tracking spread across many calendars, apps and trackers is hard to keep updated. So there are two ways a planner can fail. It can be too big to maintain, or too thin so you end up on sticky notes and separate apps. This bundle tries to sit between those: small pages, but enough of them to cover a day.

A quick-start sheet plus ten single-job pages:

  3. Weekly Habit Tracker

  5. Evening Wind-Down

  7. Grocery List
  8. Medication & Task Tracker (a place to write things down, nothing more)
  9. Dopamine Menu
  10. Micro Chore Checklist

You get one combined PDF and a zip of separate files, so you can print the whole set or only today's page.

How it is meant to be used

  • Print the one page you need today. Ignore the rest until you want them.
  • Nothing is linked to anything else, so skipping a day leaves nothing to catch up on.
  • Reprint a page whenever you like.

Who it is not for

  • If you love a big, detailed planner and keep it up happily, you do not need this.
  • If you want reminders, syncing or alarms, this is paper. Use an app for that. Our comparison guide covers when an app is the better pick.
  • It is printable PDF files only. You need a printer or a tablet to write on.
